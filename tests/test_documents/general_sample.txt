The history of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) began in 1969 under the vision of Dr. Vikram Sarabhai. 
Since its inception, ISRO has launched numerous satellites and space missions that have contributed significantly 
to India's technological development. The Chandrayaan-3 mission in 2023 successfully landed on the lunar south pole, 
making India the fourth country to achieve a soft landing on the Moon.

ISRO's satellite launch vehicles, including the PSLV and GSLV, have become among the most reliable in the world. 
The organisation has also launched satellites for other countries, establishing India as a global leader in 
affordable space technology. Future missions include Gaganyaan, India's first human spaceflight programme, 
and Aditya-L1, a solar observation mission.

ISRO continues to collaborate with international space agencies and universities to advance scientific research 
and technology transfer. The organisation's budget has grown steadily over the past decade, reflecting the 
government's commitment to space exploration and satellite-based services for agriculture, weather forecasting, 
and disaster management.
